Where this started

Everyone had an AI tool. None of them sounded like us.

Last year, AI went from novelty to daily habit across the company. I watched most of our team reach for it to write and edit content — each on their own paid subscription, each with no guardrails. The output was fast, but it didn't sound like Financial Finesse, and we had no way to regulate what any of it said. In a business built on financial trust, that's a real risk.

So I asked a simple question: what if there was one tool, built on our voice standards, our tone, and our expertise — so everything that came out of it matched the company by default? People would stop reaching for tools we couldn't control, and every piece would be on-brand the moment it was generated.

That idea became the Global Content Studio. Writers went from spending hours on a piece to minutes.

The turn

Then we decided to go global.

Once the studio proved itself for writing, I saw where the company was headed: every market, every country. Localizing our library the traditional way would have meant hiring one in-country specialist per market, then having each of them translate, culturally adapt, and QA every single item. It was slow, expensive, and it didn't scale.

The scale problem

The solution

From a writing tool to a localization engine.

I pivoted the studio from writing content to writing and localizing it. It can take an existing article or write a brand-new one, then adapt it for any country in the world — one at a time or in bulk. Not a translation. A rewrite in the correct tone, financial structure, customs, and culture.

01

Brand-trained AI. Grounded in Financial Finesse voice pillars, compliance rules, and content schemas, running through the Anthropic API. Every draft comes back already sounding like us — not like generic AI.

02

LLM Wiki — our source of truth. For each country, I built a knowledge base the model pulls from. Instead of scraping the open web for financial facts, the AI draws from vetted, country-specific sources we control. That's what makes localization accurate instead of a guess.

03

True localization, scored. Each output is rewritten for the correct tone, financial structure, customs, ways of life, religion, and culture — then scored at least 0.9 on each dimension. The tool explains why it changed what it changed, and flags anything it's unsure about for a human to check.

04

Human in the loop, always. Every article gets human QA before it ships. A coach reviews and signs off on what the system flags. The AI does the heavy lifting; a person always has the final say.

Inside Atlas

What happens to every article.

I designed an 8-step pipeline — Atlas — that every article moves through, whether it's brand-new or being localized. It starts with a fit check that decides whether the piece even belongs in that market, then runs eight controlled steps before anything can publish.

Gate · Fit check
Does this topic even apply here?

Before anything runs, we ask whether the topic even applies in this market. If an article is built around something that doesn't exist there, we don't localize it.

Why it matters: we never localize advice that doesn't fit the reader's country.

01
Triage

Size up how much the article needs to change to work in the new market, so simple pieces move fast and complex ones get the attention they need.

Why it matters: effort goes where it's actually needed.

02
Research the local facts

Pull the real facts for that country from LLM Wiki: local programs, limits, and rules. Anything time-sensitive is flagged to be verified before it ships.

Why it matters: local facts come from a source of truth we control, not a web search.

03
Rewrite for the market

Rewrite the article in the local language and our coach voice. For English-speaking markets, we swap the regional facts rather than translate.

Why it matters: it reads as locally authored, not as raw machine translation.

04
Cultural review

Check that tone, examples, and expressions fit the culture, customs, and ways of life of the market.

Why it matters: we catch wording that reads as foreign or off-brand before a human ever sees it.

05
Numbers & compliance

Verify every figure, rate, and legal statement against the market, and flag anything that needs a closer look.

Why it matters: in financial content, a wrong number is a real risk.

06
The guardrail

Automatically scrub every localized article of US-specific links and US-only terms, swapping them for the local equivalent. Anything the system can't safely fix is blocked from publishing until a person handles it.

Why it matters: a US reference never slips through unseen.

07
Human review

A financial coach reviews everything the system flagged and approves the article. Every localized piece waits here for a person.

Why it matters: nothing publishes itself — a human always has the final say.

08
Publish

Once approved, the article is released to our shared library for that market, where everyone sees the same reviewed version.

Why it matters: one controlled, consistent release across every market we serve.

Confidence scoring runs across the pipeline: each output is rated at least 0.9 on tone, financial structure, customs, culture, and compliance — with reasons for every change and a flag on anything uncertain.

Learnings

What I took away from building it.

01
A source of truth beats a smarter model.

The LLM Wiki was the unlock. Giving the AI vetted, country-specific knowledge to pull from is what turned localization from a plausible guess into something we could actually trust and ship.

02
Keep the human where the stakes are.

Automating the work isn't the same as removing the person. Designing the pipeline so a coach signs off on every flagged article is what made the speed safe in a financial context.

03
Building it myself made it better.

There was no spec, no handoff, no waiting on a dev sprint. Designing and building in one loop meant I could feel every rough edge in the pipeline and fix it the same day. That's the real thesis: a designer who can build ships a better product, faster.

Final Takeaway

Generic AI gives you words. A studio trained on your voice gives you a team that ships. A designer who can build it gives you both — at a speed that changes what your company can go after.
