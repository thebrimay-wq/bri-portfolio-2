Case studyFinancial Finesse2026Internal tool, v1 built solo

An internal tool nobody asked for. Now it runs our content in 25 countries.

Writers were drafting company content on their own AI tools, with no shared voice and no record of what was checked. I designed and coded the first version of a studio that drafts in our voice, flags every claim it can't source, and leaves the final call to a local reviewer. Then two engineers and I rebuilt it twice.

01 · At a glance

The short version

I named the risk, built the first version myself, and carried it through two rebuilds as the company made it part of its platform.

The problem

Drafts drifted off-voice, and nobody recorded which claims had been checked. Then our CEO set her sights on new markets.

The bet

A studio that already knows our voice, our compliance rules, and each market's facts, with a person signing off on everything.

My role

I designed and coded the first version. After that, I designed and built the review screens while two engineers rebuilt the engine.

02 · The review console

An internal tool for 35 localizers in 26 countries

None of them are technical. Each sees only their market, reviews every flag the pipeline raised on a claim it was unsure of, and edits right in the text.

What they do

Act on each flag with Apply, Fix myself, or Dismiss, then approve. Only a publisher can release, and every article keeps its history.

One review, not two

Every non-English article carries an English version that updates as the localizer edits, so nobody reviews the same article twice.

Weekly, with me

I'm the main contact for all 35. We meet every week to move each market through localizing, and I use their feedback to tune the pipeline's accuracy.

03 · v1, built by me

I built the first version myself

Every draft starts with our voice, our compliance rules, and the right format. If the AI can't point to a source for a fact, it flags it for a person.

04 · Going global

Translating wasn't enough, so we rewrite for each country

"Contribute to your 401(k)" in French is still wrong when every word is right. So each article gets checked for fit, researched locally, rewritten, double-checked, and signed off by a local reviewer.

05 · Decisions

Four calls I'd make again

Our own fact library, not the open web

Financial facts have to be ours. The web can add to the library, but it never replaces it.

Five careful passes, not one fast one

One quick pass kept US habits no check could catch. Five passes cost more and caught them.

The AI's score doesn't decide what ships

It only decides what a reviewer sees first. There's no auto-publish setting, on purpose.

Approved means approved

Once a person approves an article, the tool won't touch it again unless someone asks for that article by name.

06 · What broke

It worked, until it didn't

A tool one person built hit its limits. I wrote down four problems, and they became the plan for the next version.

01Lost edits

A re-run replaced work a localizer had corrected. On three reviewed articles, none of the reviewed sentences survived.

02Flags hard to act on

One Bulgarian run raised 630 flags on 445 articles, 78% of them "check this number", in a separate tab with no action.

03No audit trail

A name prompt in the browser stood in for sign-in. Nobody could say who approved what.

04Slow at scale

Pages timed out as the library grew.

07 · Moving in

Then we moved it into our CMS, so there's one login

The engineers rebuilt the engine, and I designed and built the review screens. Then we brought the whole thing into the CMS our team already uses, so reviewers sign in once, with the same roles as everything else.

Lost editsYour edits are safe

A reviewer's text is saved on its own, and an out-of-date save can't overwrite it.

FlagsPlain words, clear actions

"verification:freshness" became "Check this number", with Apply, Fix myself, and Dismiss.

Who did whatOne sign-in, real roles

Reviewers use their work sign-in and only see their country.

08 · Designing the AI

A person always makes the final call

We never trained a model. The AI writes from a fact library we keep for each market, a second pass checks its work, and a person approves it.

A second read

A second AI pass reads each draft like a local would and sends it back if something's off. A simple check then removes US-only links and terms.

It knows what doesn't fit

Of 75 articles the tool held back from the UK, 62 truly didn't apply there.
