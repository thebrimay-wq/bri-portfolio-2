The Problem

Everyone had an AI tool. None of them sounded like us — and then we decided to go global.

Last year, AI went from novelty to daily habit across the company. Most of the team reached for it to write and edit content — each on their own paid subscription, each with no guardrails. The output was fast, but it didn't sound like Financial Finesse, and we had no way to regulate what any of it said. In a business built on financial trust, that's a real risk.

Then the company set its sights on every market. Localizing our library the traditional way would have meant hiring one in-country specialist per market, then having each of them translate, culturally adapt, and QA every single item — slow, expensive, and impossible to scale.

~650 pieces × 25 countries = 16,250 localized outputs needed — and 25 in-country localizers to recruit and manage.
Financial advice changes by country: tax structures, retirement systems, and regulations are all different.
Tone, customs, religion, and ways of life all shape how money is discussed — a literal translation would be technically correct and completely wrong for the reader.
Getting it wrong in a financial context isn't a typo — it's a liability.

The Solution

From a writing tool to a localization engine

I pivoted the studio from writing content to writing and localizing it. It can take an existing article or write a brand-new one, then adapt it for any country in the world — one at a time or in bulk. Not a translation. A rewrite in the correct tone, financial structure, customs, and culture.

01

Brand-trained AI. Grounded in Financial Finesse voice pillars, compliance rules, and content schemas, running through the Anthropic API. Every draft comes back already sounding like us — not like generic AI.

02

LLM Wiki — our source of truth. For each country, I built a knowledge base the model pulls from. Instead of scraping the open web for financial facts, the AI draws from vetted, country-specific sources we control. That's what makes localization accurate instead of a guess.

03

True localization, scored. Each output is rewritten for the correct tone, financial structure, customs, ways of life, religion, and culture — then scored at least 0.9 on each dimension. The tool explains why it changed what it changed, and flags anything it's unsure about for a human to check.

04

Human in the loop, always. Every article gets human QA before it ships. A coach reviews and signs off on what the system flags. The AI does the heavy lifting; a person always has the final say.

The Pipeline

Inside Atlas — what happens to every article

Every article moves through Atlas, an 8-step pipeline — whether it's brand-new or being localized. It starts with a fit check that decides whether the piece even belongs in that market (if it's built around something that doesn't exist there, we don't localize it), then runs eight controlled steps. Confidence scoring runs across the whole pipeline: each output is rated at least 0.9 on tone, financial structure, customs, culture, and compliance.

Atlas.pngThe Atlas pipeline — step tracker · confidence scores · flags
01

Triage. Size up how much the article needs to change to work in the new market, so simple pieces move fast and complex ones get the attention they need.Why it matters: effort goes where it's actually needed.

02

Research the local facts. Pull the real facts for that country from LLM Wiki — local programs, limits, and rules. Anything time-sensitive is flagged to be verified before it ships.Why it matters: local facts come from a source of truth we control, not a web search.

03

Rewrite for the market. Rewrite the article in the local language and our coach voice. For English-speaking markets, we swap the regional facts rather than translate.Why it matters: it reads as locally authored, not as raw machine translation.

04

Cultural review. Check that tone, examples, and expressions fit the culture, customs, and ways of life of the market.Why it matters: we catch wording that reads as foreign or off-brand before a human ever sees it.

05

Numbers & compliance. Verify every figure, rate, and legal statement against the market, and flag anything that needs a closer look.Why it matters: in financial content, a wrong number is a real risk.

06

The guardrail. Automatically scrub every localized article of US-specific links and US-only terms, swapping them for the local equivalent. Anything the system can't safely fix is blocked from publishing until a person handles it.Why it matters: a US reference never slips through unseen.

07

Human review. A financial coach reviews everything the system flagged and approves the article. Every localized piece waits here for a person.Why it matters: nothing publishes itself — a human always has the final say.

08

Publish. Once approved, the article is released to our shared library for that market, where everyone sees the same reviewed version.Why it matters: one controlled, consistent release across every market we serve.

Process

Designed and built solo. Zero to one, no dev team.

I built this entire app myself with Claude Code — from zero to one, with no engineering help. The first working version came together in about two weeks; I spent the four after that tuning the pipeline until every step held up under real content. A traditional version would have needed a PM, a designer, and a multi-person dev pod running for months. I did it as one designer who can build — directing AI, shipping production code.
